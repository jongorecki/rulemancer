Wayward Angel's last ability is removed by Turn to Frog in layer 6, but since it's already started to apply in layer 5 to change its color to black, it will continue doing so in later layers. Turn to Frog has the earlier timestamp, so it makes Wayward Angel blue before it becomes black in layer 5 and removes Wayward Angel's abilities in layer 6 before it gains trample and "At the beginning of your upkeep, sacrifice a creature".

When Aimee then turns Wayward Angel face up, the permanent receives a *new* timestamp at that moment [613.7f]. - **Color (layer 5):** Turn to Frog makes it blue [613.1e]; Threshold's "is black" is applied later in the same layer, so Wayward Angel is black only — not blue, and not white-and-black [Wayward Angel ruling #1].

### CELL c0116

**Question**
Asa controls [Library of Leng] and is discarding a card due to [Azra Oddsmaker]'s ability. Can they put the discarded card on top of their library? If so, what happens?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
Yes. Discarding a cost to the ability is a cost [118.12], but that doesn't stop it from being an effect as well. Library of Leng only changes where the card moves to, not that it was discarded, so the cost was paid and the remainder of Azra Oddsmaker's ability will resolve as normal.

**CANDIDATE**
No — Asa must put the discarded card into their graveyard. Library of Leng doesn't apply to Azra Oddsmaker's discard, because that discard is a cost, not an effect.

Why: Azra Oddsmaker's triggered ability reads "you may discard a card. If you do, choose a creature..." That's exactly the template covered by rule 118.12 — "[A player] may [do something]. If [that player] [does]..." — and that rule states the "[do something]" action is a cost, paid as the triggered ability resolves. Library of Leng's replacement only works "If an effect causes you to discard a card," and its ruling is explicit that you can't use it when you discard as a cost, because costs aren't effects (rule 609.1 confirms an effect is something produced by a resolving spell/ability or a static ability, and text itself is never an effect) [Library of Leng ruling #5].

What does happen: if Asa chooses to discard, the card goes from hand to graveyard, Asa has paid the cost, and Azra Oddsmaker's ability continues — Asa chooses a creature, and whenever that creature deals combat damage to a player this turn, Asa draws two cards. Note that under rule 118.12 the "If you do" clause checks whether the player started to pay the cost, so even if some other effect altered what actually happened during the discard, the cost would still count as paid (compare rule 118.11). Library of Leng's other ability — Asa has no maximum hand size — is unaffected and still applies.

If instead the discard came from an effect that just tells you to discard (e.g., a resolving spell's instruction, not an "If you do" cost), Library of Leng would let Asa put that card on top of their library instead, and it would still count as a discard for anything that triggers on discarding [Library of Leng ruling #4].

Player count doesn't matter here: nothing in this interaction depends on the number of opponents, so it works identically in a two-player game and at a multiplayer table.

**Question**
Aimee casts [Animate Dead] targeting [Scalebane's Elite] in their graveyard. What happens?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
Both permanents end up in the graveyard.

Scalebane's Elite's protection ability doesn't function in the graveyard, so it's a legal target for Animate Dead. As Animate Dead's trigger resolves, first Animate Dead's enchant ability changes to "enchant creature put onto the battlefield with Animate Dead". Then Scalebane's Elite is returned to the battlefield. At this point the next instruction is to attach Animate Dead to Scalebane's Elite, but this is illegal to do because now that it's on the battlefield, Scalebane's Elite's protection ability is active.

After the triggered ability from Animate Dead has finished resolving, the game will check state-based actions and see that Animate Dead is an Aura that isn't enchanting anything, so it's put into its owner's graveyard.. This causes Animate Dead's leaves-the-battlefield ability to trigger, sacrificing Scalebane's Elite.

**CANDIDATE**
Aimee's Animate Dead resolves normally and returns Scalebane's Elite to the battlefield under her control, but Animate Dead cannot stay attached to it, and the delayed trigger then forces Aimee to sacrifice Scalebane's Elite. Here's the full sequence: Animate Dead is cast targeting Scalebane's Elite in the graveyard. Scalebane's Elite's "Protection from black" is a static ability that, per [Animate Dead ruling #4], functions only on the battlefield unless otherwise specified, so while Scalebane's Elite is a card in the graveyard it has no protection and is a fully legal target — Animate Dead resolves as a normal Aura spell (as noted in [Animate Dead ruling #1], Animate Dead is an Aura that enters attached to the graveyard card). Per [701.3b], an effect that tries to attach an Aura to something it can't legally enchant simply fails — the Aura doesn't move. As stated in [Animate Dead ruling #0], when the creature returned this way can't legally be enchanted by Animate Dead (e.g., due to protection from black), Animate Dead is unable to attach to it and is put into its owner's graveyard as a state-based action, per [704.5m] and [303.4c] (an Aura not attached to a legal object/player, or not attached at all, is put into its owner's graveyard). That causes Animate Dead's delayed "when this Aura leaves the battlefield" trigger to go on the stack; when it resolves, since Scalebane's Elite is still on the battlefield, its controller (Aimee) must sacrifice it, exactly as described in [Animate Dead ruling #0]. Net result: Scalebane's Elite briefly returns to the battlefield under Aimee's control, then Animate Dead goes to the graveyard, and Aimee is forced to sacrifice Scalebane's Elite.

### CELL c0119

**Question**
Alexis would like to choose colorless for [Regicide]. Can they?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
No. Colorless is not a color.

**CANDIDATE**
No. When Regicide's draft ability asks each of the three players to choose a color, "colorless" isn't a legal choice — a player asked to choose a color must pick one of the five colors: white, blue, black, red, or green [105.4]. "Multicolored" is likewise not a color [105.4], and there are exactly five colors in the game [105.1].

So Alexis must name one of white, blue, black, red, or green when Regicide is drafted, and the three colors chosen (by the player to Alexis's right, by Alexis, and by the player to Alexis's left) must all be different from one another [Regicide ruling #6].

A related point that may be what's behind the question: even if Alexis could somehow have "colorless" in the mix, it wouldn't help — Regicide can't target colorless creatures [Regicide ruling #1]. A colorless object simply has no color [105.2c], so it can never be "one or more of the colors chosen." Conversely, Regicide's target may have extra colors beyond those chosen, as long as it's at least one of the chosen colors [Regicide ruling #3].

On player count: Regicide's draft ability inherently references the players to your right and left at the draft table, so it assumes a draft pod rather than a two-player game; regardless of how many players are seated, the restriction to the five colors is the same for each chooser.

**Question**
In Aspen's draw step, they tap [Vault of Catlacan] for 8 blue mana and cast [Time Stop]. Aspen discards a [Guile] to hand size. Can they use the two remaining {U} to cast an instant?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
No. The mana was removed from the mana pool when the draw step ended.

**CANDIDATE**
Yes. Aspen gets a priority window to cast an instant with the remaining {U}{U}, because discarding Guile to hand size triggers Guile's own ability, and that trigger forces a priority window during the cleanup step that follows Time Stop's turn-ending effect.

Here's the sequence: Aspen casts Time Stop ({4}{U}{U}) using 6 of the 8 blue mana produced by Vault of Catlacan, leaving 2 {U} floating. Time Stop's effect ends the turn, which is resolved using the special process in [724.1] and [724.1f] rather than normal resolution: spells/abilities are exiled, the active player (Aspen) discards down to maximum hand size, damage heals, and 'until end of turn' effects end. No player gets priority during this process itself.

Because Aspen discards Guile as part of that hand-size reduction, Guile goes to the graveyard, which triggers its own ability: 'When Guile is put into a graveyard from anywhere, shuffle it into its owner's library' (Guile). Per [724.1f], any triggered ability that has triggered since the end-the-turn process began is put onto the stack during the cleanup step, and then the active player gets priority and players can cast spells and activate abilities — this mirrors the general cleanup-step exception in [514.3] and [514.3a]. So instead of the turn simply ending with no priority, Aspen's Guile-shuffle trigger goes on the stack in the cleanup step, Aspen receives priority, and can use the 2 {U} still in their mana pool to cast an instant at that time (before or after letting the Guile trigger resolve, as they choose, since they have priority with the trigger on the stack).

Note that after this priority window closes (stack empties, all players pass), [724.1f] says there will be another cleanup step before the turn finally ends — so if nothing else triggers, the turn ends normally after that second cleanup step.

This scenario is inherently two-player-style priority as described, but the rules given don't flag any multiplayer-specific difference in how the cleanup-step priority window works, so the same reasoning applies regardless of the number of players.

**Question**
Ariel controls [Frostboil Snarl] and [Blood Moon]. They cast [Prismatic Omen]. After it resolves, what land types does Frostboil Snarl have?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
All 5. Prismatic Omen and Blood Moon's effects are not dependent on each other, so they are are applied in timestamp order.

**CANDIDATE**
Frostboil Snarl has all five basic land types: Plains, Island, Swamp, Mountain, and Forest.

Why: both effects apply in layer 4 (type-changing effects), in timestamp order (CR 613.1d).

1. Blood Moon ("Nonbasic lands are Mountains") applies first. Frostboil Snarl is nonbasic (it has no "basic" supertype, CR 305.8/205.4c), so its land subtypes are set to just Mountain. Per CR 305.7 and 205.1a, setting a land's subtype to a basic land type wipes its old land types and the abilities from its rules text, replacing them with "{T}: Add {R}" ([Blood Moon ruling #2]). So its "As this land enters..." ability and "{T}: Add {U} or {R}" are gone.
2. Prismatic Omen then applies with the later timestamp. It adds every basic land type "in addition to their other types," so per CR 305.7's last sentence and CR 205.1b, Frostboil Snarl keeps Mountain and gains Plains, Island, Swamp, and Forest, plus the mana ability of each ([Prismatic Omen ruling #0]).

Net result: land types Plains, Island, Swamp, Mountain, Forest; mana abilities "{T}: Add {W}", "{T}: Add {U}", "{T}: Add {B}", "{T}: Add {R}", "{T}: Add {G}". Its name is still Frostboil Snarl, and it is still a nonbasic land — neither effect changes names or supertypes ([Blood Moon ruling #1], [Prismatic Omen ruling #1], CR 205.4b, CR 305.8). Because it remains nonbasic, Blood Moon keeps applying to it; the two effects coexist rather than one shutting off the other.

Assumption on timing: Frostboil Snarl and Blood Moon were already on the battlefield and Prismatic Omen resolved afterward. If instead Blood Moon had entered after Prismatic Omen, Blood Moon would have the later timestamp and would set Frostboil Snarl's subtypes to Mountain only (Prismatic Omen's earlier addition would be overwritten in layer 4).

Player count doesn't matter here: both enchantments are Ariel's own permanents, and Blood Moon affects all nonbasic lands regardless of how many opponents are at the table.

### CELL c0127

**Question**
Arely equips [Barbed Batterfist] onto [Champion of the Flame]. Does Champion of the Flame die?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
No, Champion of the Flame will be a 4/2 and not die. All continuous effects are applied instantly before state based actions are checked.

**CANDIDATE**
No — Champion of the Flame does not die. With Barbed Batterfist attached, Champion of the Flame is a 4/2.

The math: Champion of the Flame is printed 1/1. Its own static ability gives it +2/+2 for each Aura and Equipment attached to it, and Barbed Batterfist is one Equipment attached to it, so that's +2/+2. Barbed Batterfist itself says the equipped creature gets +1/-1. Both are power/toughness modifications from static abilities, which apply together in layer 7c; they're cumulative and the order they're applied in doesn't matter (see [613.5], where multiple layer 7c effects simply add together). 1/1 + 2/+2 + 1/-1 = 4/2.

Since Champion of the Flame's toughness is 2 (greater than 0), the state-based action check that happens whenever a player would receive priority finds nothing to do to it ([704.3]). It only dies if it has 2 or more damage marked on it at that check — but a 1/1 Champion of the Flame with any damage marked would already have been destroyed before you equipped, so in the normal case there's no marked damage and it survives.

Timing note: I'm assuming Barbed Batterfist is attached with no damage already marked on Champion of the Flame. If Champion of the Flame somehow already had damage marked (for example, it was pumped earlier in the turn and took 2+ damage), attaching Barbed Batterfist raises its toughness to 2 but marked damage stays on it, and it would be destroyed at the next state-based action check if that damage is 2 or more ([704.3]).

The converse warning on Champion of the Flame's ruling matters later: if Barbed Batterfist is unattached, destroyed, or moved to another creature during the turn, Champion of the Flame drops back toward 1/1 and previously nonlethal marked damage can become lethal [Champion of the Flame ruling #1].

One aside on Barbed Batterfist: if it was equipping the 2/2 Rebel token it created, you can pay its equip cost of {1} (as a sorcery) to move it to Champion of the Flame; the Rebel token stays on the battlefield and returns to 2/2 [Barbed Batterfist ruling #1].

The provided rules here are not player-count dependent, so this answer is the same in a two-player game or at a multiplayer table.

### CELL c0128

**Question**
Autumn, Bethany, and Chaim started playing a multiplayer game. Chaim lost the game, and then Autumn restarted the game with [Karn Liberated]. Will Autumn draw a card on the first turn of the restarted game?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
No. The game began with 2 players, so it is a two-player game.. Autumn will skip their draw step.

**CANDIDATE**
No — Autumn will not draw a card during the draw step of the first turn of the restarted game (beyond the opening hand of seven).

Why: Karn Liberated's −14 ability restarts the game. A restarted game immediately ends the old game and all players in it begin a new game following the normal starting-the-game procedures [727.1]. The Karn Liberated ruling states that any player who left the game before it was restarted won't be involved in the new game, and that the player who controlled the restarting ability is the starting player in the new game. Since Chaim had already lost (left) the game, only Autumn and Bethany are in the new game, and Autumn plays first.

A "two-player game" is defined as a game that begins with only two players [100.1a], and a multiplayer game is one that begins with more than two players [800.1]. The restarted game begins with just Autumn and Bethany, so it is a two-player game, not a multiplayer game — even though the original game was multiplayer. In a two-player game, the player who plays first skips the draw step of their first turn [103.8a]. Autumn is that player, so Autumn skips the draw step and draws no card on turn one.

Contrast: had Chaim still been in the game at the moment of the restart, the new game would begin with three players and would be a multiplayer game [800.1], so rule 103.8a's skip wouldn't apply and Autumn would draw normally on the first turn.

Autumn does still shuffle, draw an opening hand of seven, and may mulligan as normal per the Karn Liberated ruling, and Karn Liberated's ability finishes resolving (putting the exiled non-Aura permanent cards onto the battlefield) after the pregame procedure but before the first turn.

### CELL c0129

**Question**
Ari controls [Bludgeon Brawl] and [Soldevi Digger]. They cast [Titania's Song]. Once it resolves, what are the characteristics of Soldevi Digger?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
It's a 2/2 artifact creature with no abilities. Applying Titania's Song would cause Bludgeon Brawl to no longer apply (since Soldevi Digger would be a creature). However, applying Bludgeon Brawl would not change how Titania's Song's effect is applied, so Bludgeon Brawl is dependent on Titania's Song. Titania's Song applies first, and then Bludgeon Brawl won't affect Soldevi Digger since it's a creature.

**CANDIDATE**
Soldevi Digger ends up as a colorless 2/2 artifact creature — Equipment with no abilities at all.

How the layers get there (CR 613.1a, 613.6, 613.7 — same timestamp order in each layer, no dependency applies):

- Layer 4 (types/subtypes): Bludgeon Brawl has the earlier timestamp, so it applies first and makes Soldevi Digger an Equipment (it qualified: at that point it was a noncreature, non-Equipment artifact). Then Titania's Song makes it an artifact creature. Result: Artifact Creature — Equipment.
- Layer 6 (abilities): Bludgeon Brawl first grants "equip {2}" and "Equipped creature gets +2/+0" (X = Soldevi Digger's mana value, which is 2 — that's {2} generic to equip). Then Titania's Song, with the later timestamp, removes all abilities — including Soldevi Digger's printed "{2}: Put the top card of your graveyard on the bottom of your library" and the two abilities Bludgeon Brawl just granted. Result: no abilities.
- Layer 7b: Titania's Song sets power and toughness each equal to mana value, so 2/2. Note the mana value used is still 2 ({2} generic on the printed card); Bludgeon Brawl doesn't change mana cost.
- Color: nothing changes it, so Soldevi Digger stays colorless (CR 105.2 — no colored mana symbols in its cost, no color indicator or color-defining ability).

Key point about CR 613.6: once Bludgeon Brawl's effect starts applying in layer 4, it keeps applying to Soldevi Digger in layer 6 even though by then Soldevi Digger is a creature and an Equipment — but its granted abilities are stripped anyway because Titania's Song's ability-removal has the later timestamp.

Timing assumption: I'm assuming Bludgeon Brawl was already on the battlefield before Titania's Song resolved (as stated). If instead Titania's Song had the earlier timestamp, Bludgeon Brawl would then no longer apply — Soldevi Digger would be a creature, and Bludgeon Brawl only affects noncreature artifacts — so it would be a 2/2 artifact creature with no abilities and no Equipment subtype. Also, if Titania's Song later leaves the battlefield, its own text says the effect continues until end of turn.

This analysis doesn't depend on the number of players; both cards affect artifacts regardless of controller, and the provided rules contain nothing player-count-specific here.

### CELL c0130

**Question**
Noah activates [Thespian's Stage] targeting a [Dark Depths] with ten ice counters on it. What happens?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
Thespian's Stage becomes a copy of Dark Depths. Thespian's Stage has no ice counters on it, so its state trigger triggers, but doesn't go on the stack yet. Then state-based actions are checked and Noah has to put either Dark Depths or Thespian's Stage into their graveyard. Then Thespian's Stage's trigger goes on the stack, and if Thespian's Stage is still on the battlefield during the resolution of the trigger, Noah sacrifices it and creates a 20/20 Marit Lage token.

**CANDIDATE**
Thespian's Stage becomes a copy of Dark Depths, but because Thespian's Stage was already on the battlefield when the copy effect applies, it does NOT get Dark Depths' ten ice counters, and its "When Dark Depths has no ice counters on it, sacrifice it" ability will immediately be true, causing that ability to trigger and, once it resolves, Noah sacrifices Thespian's Stage and creates Marit Lage, a legendary 20/20 black Avatar creature token with flying and indestructible.

Walking through it: Thespian's Stage's activated ability ("{2}, {T}: This land becomes a copy of target land, except it has this ability") makes it a copy of the targeted Dark Depths under rule [707.5] and [613.2a] (copiable effects apply in layer 1a). However, per Thespian's Stage's own ruling, "No enters-the-battlefield abilities of the land Thespian's Stage is copying will trigger... Thespian's Stage was already on the battlefield. Similarly, no 'as this land enters the battlefield' or 'this land enters the battlefield with' effects, such as that of Dark Depths, will apply" [Thespian's Stage ruling #1]. Dark Depths' text "Dark Depths enters with ten ice counters on it" is exactly such an enters-with effect, so it simply does not apply to Thespian's Stage — this is Dark Depths' printed text being overridden by the specific circumstance that Thespian's Stage never 'enters' as part of this transformation, consistent with [Thespian's Stage ruling #1] and [Thespian's Stage ruling #3] ("Counters and other effects aren't copied"). This is also consistent with the general rule that counters are never part of an object's copiable values and don't transfer between objects [122.2].

So the moment the copy effect resolves, Thespian's Stage is now a Legendary Snow Land named Dark Depths with zero ice counters on it, plus Thespian's Stage's own copying ability (retained per its text "except it has this ability"). Dark Depths' last ability, "When Dark Depths has no ice counters on it, sacrifice it. If you do, create Marit Lage...", is a state-trigger, per [Dark Depths ruling #1], and its trigger condition (no ice counters) is already true, so it triggers as soon as state-based actions/triggers are checked after the copy effect takes hold. When that triggered ability resolves, Noah sacrifices the permanent (which is now the Thespian's Stage/Dark Depths copy) and creates Marit Lage, a legendary 20/20 black Avatar creature token with flying and indestructible, exactly as printed on Dark Depths.

One side note: Thespian's Stage does not become untapped by this copy effect (it stays tapped from being tapped to activate its ability), per [Thespian's Stage ruling #2], but this is irrelevant since the permanent is about to be sacrificed anyway. Also, if Noah already controlled the original Dark Depths (still with its own counters, since that Dark Depths itself is untouched — only the copy lacks counters), the legend rule could matter, but the question only describes one Dark Depths, so that scenario doesn't apply here; if Noah did control both, he'd need to consider [Dark Depths ruling #2]-style concerns about the permanent leaving the battlefield before the trigger resolves, but no such conflict is indicated in this scenario.

### CELL c0131

**Question**
Ariel controls [Bastion Inventor] and an [Oblivion Ring] that exiled Noelle's [Ghostly Possession]. Noelle casts [Serene Offering] targeting Oblivion Ring. What happens as the Ghostly Possession returns to the battlefield?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
It enchants Bastion Inventor. Auras entering the battlefield from a zone other than the stack must enchant a legal object. It doesn't target anything, so hexproof doesn't matter.

**CANDIDATE**
Ghostly Possession comes back onto the battlefield under Noelle's control (its owner's control), and Noelle chooses what it enchants as it enters. Because it isn't targeting anything, Bastion Inventor's hexproof does not stop it — hexproof only prevents Bastion Inventor from being the target of spells or abilities Noelle's opponents control, and this Aura placement doesn't target at all. So if Bastion Inventor is the only creature available, Noelle may (and, if it's the only legal choice, must) attach Ghostly Possession to Bastion Inventor, giving it flying and preventing all combat damage dealt to and by it.

Sequence: Serene Offering destroys Oblivion Ring (Noelle gains 3 life, Oblivion Ring's mana value). Oblivion Ring's second triggered ability triggers on leaving the battlefield and, on resolution, returns the exiled Ghostly Possession to the battlefield under its owner's control [Oblivion Ring ruling #2]. Since it's entering by an effect rather than by resolving as an Aura spell and the effect doesn't specify what it enchants, its controller chooses a legal object per its enchant ability, "Enchant creature" [303.4f]. If there is no legal creature for Ghostly Possession to enchant at that time, it isn't put onto the battlefield and remains exiled forever [303.4g], [Oblivion Ring ruling #2]. Afterward, if the enchanted creature ever becomes an illegal object for it, Ghostly Possession is put into its owner's graveyard as a state-based action [704.5m], [303.4c].

Timing note: I'm assuming Oblivion Ring's exile ability had already resolved, so Ghostly Possession was genuinely in exile. If Oblivion Ring had been destroyed before its first ability resolved, the return trigger would find nothing and the target would be exiled permanently [Oblivion Ring ruling #0].

This works the same at a two-player table or in multiplayer, except that in multiplayer an Aura can't enchant an object or player outside its controller's range of influence [801.8].

### CELL c0132

**Question**
Niko controls [Urza's Saga] on chapter 2. Ainsley casts [Magus of the Moon]. What happens to Urza's Saga?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
It becomes an Enchantment Land - Saga Mountain with an ability to tap for {R}, an ability to tap for {1}, an ability to create Constructs, and no other abilities.

Since it no longer has any chapter abilities, it will remain at 2 lore counters and will not receive any more in future turns. It is not sacrificed.

**CANDIDATE**
Urza's Saga is put into Niko's graveyard immediately — Niko sacrifices it as a state-based action.

Why: Magus of the Moon says "Nonbasic lands are Mountains." That makes Urza's Saga a Mountain, which sets its land subtype to Mountain and removes its rules text (leaving "{T}: Add {R}"), so it loses its chapter I, II, and III abilities. It does not, however, lose the enchantment subtype Saga (Magus of the Moon changes land types only, and per [Magus of the Moon ruling #1] it doesn't touch names or supertypes either).

So Urza's Saga is still a Saga permanent, but it now has no chapter abilities. Rule [704.5s]/[714.4] only sacrifices a Saga that has one or more chapter abilities and enough lore counters — but [Urza's Saga ruling #1] states this case explicitly: "If Urza's Saga loses all of its chapter abilities but is still a Saga, perhaps due to a card like Blood Moon, it will immediately be sacrificed." Magus of the Moon has the same effect as Blood Moon here, so Urza's Saga is sacrificed the next time state-based actions are checked, regardless of it sitting on chapter 2.

Timing note: Magus of the Moon must resolve first. While Ainsley's Magus of the Moon is still on the stack, Urza's Saga is unaffected — Niko can still activate its chapter II Construct-making ability (and hold priority to do so) before Magus of the Moon resolves. Any Construct token already created stays; only Urza's Saga itself is sacrificed. Niko also loses the chapter III search, since chapter III will never trigger.

This works the same in a two-player game or at a multiplayer table: Magus of the Moon affects every nonbasic land in play, so every player's Urza's Saga (and any similar Saga land) is sacrificed by its own controller.

### CELL c0133

**Question**
Alessandro controls [Angel's Tomb] and no other creatures. They cast [Mirage Phalanx]. Can they pair it with Angel's Tomb?

**REFERENCE (correct)**
No. Soulbond is a triggered ability that will only go on the stack if Alessandro controls another eligible creature when Mirage Phalanx enters the battlefield. However, before Angel's Tomb's ability resolves, Alessandro doesn't control any other eligible creatures.

**CANDIDATE**
Yes — Alessandro can pair Mirage Phalanx with Angel's Tomb, but only if they arrange things so that Angel's Tomb is actually a creature at the moment the soulbond ability resolves.

Here's why: Soulbond lets a creature "be paired with another unpaired creature when either enters" [702.95b], but rule [702.95c] says that when the soulbond ability resolves, if either object that would be paired is no longer a creature (among other conditions), the pairing simply fails to happen. Angel's Tomb is normally just an artifact, not a creature — it only becomes "a 3/3 white Angel artifact creature with flying until end of turn" through its own triggered ability, which triggers "Whenever a creature you control enters" (Angel's Tomb).

When Mirage Phalanx enters the battlefield, two abilities trigger simultaneously under Alessandro's control: Mirage Phalanx's soulbond ability (because Mirage Phalanx itself entered) and Angel's Tomb's ability (because Mirage Phalanx, a creature, entered under Alessandro's control). Since Angel's Tomb is currently not a creature, and a creature can only be paired with another creature [702.95d], the pairing will fail if the soulbond trigger resolves while Angel's Tomb is still just a noncreature artifact.

However, if Alessandro puts the abilities on the stack so that Angel's Tomb's ability resolves first (so it resolves *below* the soulbond trigger, meaning it goes on the stack *after* it), Alessandro can choose to have Angel's Tomb become a 3/3 white Angel artifact creature with flying until end of turn (per Angel's Tomb's own text). Then, when Mirage Phalanx's soulbond ability resolves, Angel's Tomb is a creature, is on the battlefield, and is under Alessandro's control — so per [702.95c], the pairing succeeds, and Mirage Phalanx becomes paired with Angel's Tomb for as long as Alessandro controls both.

If instead the soulbond trigger resolves first (before Angel's Tomb's ability turns it into a creature), the pairing fails outright, since at that moment Angel's Tomb isn't a creature. So the outcome hinges entirely on the order in which Alessandro resolves these two simultaneous triggers — note that Angel's Tomb's Angel-creature status only lasts until end of turn, so the pairing will end automatically after that if Angel's Tomb reverts to a noncreature artifact and Alessandro no longer controls two creatures.
